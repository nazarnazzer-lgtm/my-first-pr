My role
Branding

The problem

A powered changing table solves a question of access. It does not, on its own, solve how it feels to be lifted onto one.

Equipment like this is sold as medical hardware, and medical hardware has a look: white plastic, chrome, hospital blue. That language tells the person on the table they are a case being managed, and it tells everyone else the room is a clinical exception rather than part of the building. The engineering was the team’s problem. The feeling was the brand’s.

The idea

Infrastructure, not equipment.

My role

On a ten-person SCAD team, I handled the brand side of it: the name, the colour and material direction, the written description, and the presentation and social that carried it.

N and R are Nkiyah and Ruby, the children of the families who worked with us through the project. We spent it learning how they actually live: their habits, what their families worry about, what makes a day good and what makes a day hard. 560 is our class number.

The colour moves the same way, away from the clinical. Warm bone with a deep teal accent, closer in feel to furniture than to a fitting.

The object

01

Powered and height-adjustable, with spring-loaded safety restraints, a slide-out storage surface, integrated power outlets and a sensory ceiling projector. It fits a standard ADA-compliant restroom without eating the circulation space, which is the practical reason buildings say no.

The detail

02

The teal only ever appears where a hand goes. It is the whole colour system in one rule: the accent marks what you touch, so the object explains itself without a single instruction label.
