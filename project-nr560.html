The problem

A powered changing table solves a question of access. It does not, on its own, solve how it feels to be lifted onto one.

Equipment like this is sold as medical hardware, and medical hardware has a look: white plastic, chrome, hospital blue. That language tells the person on the table they are a case being managed, and it tells everyone else the room is a clinical exception rather than part of the building. The engineering was the team’s problem. The feeling was the brand’s.

The idea

Infrastructure, not equipment.

What I did

I was one of ten designers on the SCAD team, and my work was the brand layer: naming, the colour and material story, the written description, and the presentation and social the project was argued with.

The palette goes the opposite way to the category. Warm bone and a deep teal, closer to considered furniture than to a hospital fitting. The name is a part number rather than a euphemism, because a lift and a drinking fountain have model numbers and nobody softens those. Both decisions point at the same claim: this is something a building provides, not a device somebody needs.

The object

01

Powered and height-adjustable, with spring-loaded safety restraints, a slide-out storage surface, integrated power outlets and a sensory ceiling projector. It fits a standard ADA-compliant restroom without eating the circulation space, which is the practical reason buildings say no.

The detail

02

The teal only ever appears where a hand goes. It is the whole colour system in one rule: the accent marks what you touch, so the object explains itself without a single instruction label.

The award

03

Winner, Social: Inclusive & Equity Products, at the European Product Design Award 2026, in the student category.

Credited to the full SCAD team: Robert Baker, Erika Kimani, Diana Vasconcelos, Mia Cruz, Minnie Lin, Pullen Chenjerayi, William Pan, Michael Katopes, Nazar Sementsov and Innomama Dickins. Erika Kimani led the design.

The part I would defend is the refusal to make it look medical. Every instinct in this category is to signal safety by looking clinical, and clinical is exactly what makes a person feel like a problem being solved. Warmth was the harder argument to win and it is the reason the thing reads as dignity rather than compliance.
