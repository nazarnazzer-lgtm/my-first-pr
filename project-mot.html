Museums of
Tolerance

Client
Museum of Tolerance, Los Angeles
Year
2026
My role
Art Director
Scope
Template system across five document families

The problem

A template is not used by designers. It is used by a facilitator who has twenty minutes with a class and a stack of photocopies.

The five document families had each been built on their own. Headers were set in condensed capitals, text ran the full width of the letter page, and the logo turned up in a different place on each family — or, on several, not at all. Put four of them on a desk together and they read as four organisations. In a school the museum does not visit twice, the handout is the institution in the room.

The idea

Set it like something meant to be read, not something meant to be obeyed.

Capitals strip the shape off a word, which is what makes them slow. The headers came down into mixed case and into a serif — a quieter voice for material about genocide, and a faster one for anybody skimming it before a bell. Under that: the lockup in the same corner on every page over a single rule, one blue carrying keylines, callouts and numerals, and a measure narrow enough to hold a line. Five families, one grid.

Lesson plans

01

The longest document and the one read under the most pressure. The same page, before and after: the title stops shouting, the lockup arrives, and the body finds a measure.

Teacher Preparation. Same words, same photograph, half the effort to read.

Enduring Understandings and the activity prompt. The blue numerals are the only thing allowed to be loud.

Facilitation guides

02

A short guide handed to whoever is running the room. It opens on the book being discussed, so the cover had to sit inside a frame rather than fight it.

The worksheets are the test. A pupil fills these in; nothing on the page may compete with their handwriting.

Information sheets

03

The hardest set to lay out, because the subject allows no decoration and the text cannot be cut. All that was left to change was the pace of it.

The Genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda. Capitals read as a placard; this had to read as a page.

Seminar flyers

04

The only outward-facing piece in the set: these go to teachers who have not met the museum yet. Two seminars, two grounds, one system.

Reversed out of the blue, the serif holds its weight; the condensed capitals never did.

Learning in Motion

05

The smallest piece and the only one with a number on it. The stat bar ran across the top as a band, which flattened three different facts into one stripe. Rolled into a disc it becomes the thing you look at first.

Before and after, at postcard size.

120,000+

Students reached by the programme these templates carry — seven mobile museums, five states, 66,000 miles.

The two moves, taken one at a time — the header, then the bar rolled into a disc. Neither is enough on its own; the finished card takes both.
