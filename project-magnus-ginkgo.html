The brief

Magnus needed a collection that said what the brand is actually for.

The starting point, Japanese craft against American workwear, is the most crowded corner of menswear. Done badly it turns into costume: a kimono sleeve on a chore coat, sashiko stitched onto a trucker. The collection had to earn the reference rather than wear it.

The insight

Translation is not blending. When something crosses over it keeps its structure and changes its function.

The ginkgo is the proof. It is the official tree of Tokyo, its leaf the symbol of the city. It is also one of the most planted street trees in New York. Same tree, different street, nothing given up.

The idea

Tokyo's tree already grows in New York.

The collection

The leaf became the collection's mark, hand printed in Magnus red and used as pattern, punctuation and packaging.

Hikari, 光, light. A roll on fragrance packed in a Japanese cigarette box, three bottles so it travels in pieces.

The Fitted Wool Jacket takes its silhouette from the Kataginu, the stiffened winged vest worn under samurai armour, rebuilt in a cotton wool blend so the structure reads soft. Its zip is American hardware, curved to the line of a ginkgo stem.

The Wide Leg Wool Pants carry box pleats for the silhouette and zippered back pockets for the utility, hems buttoned closed.

Red appears once per garment. One button. Never twice.

Everything was shot on grain, in American woodland and on American rock. Japanese structure standing in an American landscape, not pretending to be from there.
